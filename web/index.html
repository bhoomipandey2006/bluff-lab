06 / LEARN

Kuhn poker in two minutes

The game

Three cards: Jack, Queen, King. Both players put in one chip and get one card. The first player checks or bets one more chip. If they check, the second player can check too (showdown) or bet. A bet can be folded to or called. Highest card wins at showdown.

A strategy is twelve numbers

For each of the twelve situations you can be in, how often do you bet or call? That is all a strategy is, and it is what the sliders in Exploit me set.

Exploitability

Hand your twelve numbers to an opponent who knows them and plays the best possible counter. Exploitability is how many chips per hand that costs you beyond perfect play, shown here per 100 hands. It measures the strategy itself, not the crowd it plays against.

The seat is not fair

With perfect play from both sides, the first player still loses 5.6 chips per 100 hands on average. Moving first is a disadvantage in this game.

How the solver learns

After each deal it asks, for every choice it made, how much better the other option would have done. It shifts probability toward the options it regrets not choosing, then averages its play over all the rounds. That average gets close to unexploitable. This is regret matching inside CFR, a family of methods poker AIs are built on.

Why bluffing shows up

If you bet your King and never bet your Jack, your bet gives the hand away. An opponent who folds everything but a King takes 16.7 chips per 100 hands from the player who only bets the King and calls with Queens and Kings. The balance that cannot be exploited bets the King three times as often as the Jack. With any bluff rate from 0 to a third, the numbers can be set to fit.

Exact computations, no sampling, except the opponent's dice in Play. Every figure in the repo is listed in docs/FACTS.md.
